## 3. Three observations worth 10 minutes each

### A. When a Malayalam caller switches to English, the agent doesn't follow, and the cause is in the transcript.
What a lender's customer would feel: they switch to English and keep getting answered in Malayalam.
- **Evidence, Day 1:** in Malayalam clips, 0 of 11 English words came back in Latin script (#22). They were recognised correctly, but written in Malayalam script.
- **Evidence, Day 2:**
  - By voice, the agent kept answering in Malayalam when callers simply spoke English (T08, T09; #58).
  - In self-play, 7 of 7 English turns were answered in Malayalam, even with a prompt rule telling the model to judge by words, not script (#65).
- **Where it comes from:** the recogniser gives no language signal, so the LLM sees only Malayalam script.
- **Reference:** Sarvam has the same default (0/16), but a `codemix` mode puts English words back in Latin (16/16), so the fix exists as a product option.
- **Also matters for:** CRMs and analytics that search for "gold loan" or "renew" (#22).
- **Open questions:** is there a per-segment language tag or a Latin-for-English option on the roadmap? How do their own Inya agents handle it?

### B. The speech held up; the developer path around it is where a lender's engineers lose time.
What a developer building for a lender would feel: the first hour goes on defaults and docs, not on speech quality.
- **The speech itself, Day 2:**
  - 23 of 24 spoken digit strings were right (#59).
  - No numbers were misread across 20 calls when I listened (#62).
  - TTS first audio had a median of 779 ms (Day 2 summary).
- **The path around it:**
  - The LiveKit plugin's default TTS model is retired, and the plugin retries the error 4 times (#37).
  - Word boosting, VAD settings and partial transcripts exist in the API but aren't in the plugin (#39).
  - The plugin waits for the whole LLM reply before starting TTS (#42).
  - One dropped socket ends the call (#53).
  - The documented "verbatim" default is actually digits (ITN), on REST, streaming and per language (#14, #18, #28, #41, #49).
  - Docs and API disagree on response shapes (#31).
- **The count:** 22 rows across docs and developer experience, with no Delights (section 2).
- **Open questions:** who owns the plugins and the docs? How fast do they follow model releases? Is v3.0 (#38) the model they want new builders on?

### C. Numbers are the headline claim, and three days show where number risk actually sits in a lending call.
What a lender would feel: one wrong digit or rate is a compliance and trust problem, not a typo.
- **Strengths:**
  - A 10-digit ID and "one lakh eighty-four thousand five hundred" were right in English, in noise (#19).
  - Every amount, ID and date was read right in TTS (#33, #62).
- **Edge cases:**
  - "Nine and a half percent" came back as 9,000%, with no plausibility flag (#20).
  - One phone-line digit was misheard. Sarvam's streaming made the same error (#21, #77), so this is about phone audio, not one vendor.
- **The biggest number failure was outside Gnani.** The LLM "corrected" a caller's 4512 to 4521 because of example digits in my prompt, and a non-customer heard the loan details (#52; fixed, and confirmed in T18-R, #67).
- **The point for a lender:** number accuracy is end to end. They need read-back confirmation for IDs, plausibility checks on rates, and digit checks in code, not just a good recogniser.
- **Open questions:** does Inya ship these guardrails, or is each customer expected to build them?

## 4. The biggest product tension

Gnani's models are the strongest part of what I tried. Recognition and voice output had the most Delights and no Blockers, and the agent read every number right across 20 calls. But a builder doesn't meet the models first. They meet the docs, the defaults and the LiveKit plugin, and that's where my friction concentrated: as many rows as the models had, none of them good. The polished path, Agent Builder, is closed to individual accounts, so the evaluator who most needs a smooth first hour gets the least finished surface. Gnani seems to be building for enterprise deals, where its own team does the integration and these gaps never show. The risk is that every developer, start-up or lender's in-house team that tries Gnani alone judges it by the wrapper rather than the models, and the models are where Gnani is ahead.

## 5. What I haven't tested (so I don't overclaim)

- **Agent Builder (Inya):** never opened, because it's blocked for personal accounts (#3). Nothing here describes it.
- **Real phone calls:** only two clips were played into a call (P1, P2). The agent ran in LiveKit console mode on a laptop with headphones; there was no SIP, no 8 kHz telephony path and no real caller.
- **Hindi:**
  - The Hindi clips (C3, N3, M3) were never recorded, so there's no Day 1 Hindi accuracy.
  - Hindi appears only in the agent tests (T05, T17 and similar, 10 timed turns).
- **Other speakers:** every recording is me, with my accent and my phone. There's no diversity of age, gender, dialect or speaking speed.
- **Sample size:** 11 clips, one request each for accuracy. The Gnani vs Sarvam gap on accuracy is 6 vs 12 word errors, which is too few to call a ranking.
- **Word boosting:** I confirmed it was active (#40) but never measured whether it changes accuracy.
- **Voice cloning:** one run (#32, #35).
- **Timbre v3.0 expression tags, batch STT, load and concurrency, long calls:** none tested.
- **Cost at scale:** only derived per-call figures from the dashboard (#63); no pricing for production volumes.
- **Sarvam:**
  - No agent rebuild (step 5, optional, not run).
  - Like Gnani, all from one speaker and these 11 clips.
